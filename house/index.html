Off-grid house for Darlington Point

last checked: 2026-09-28T23:30 (UTC)

  • Crew housing: leasing a 3-bed in Darlington Point is the only decent option under $100k, about $11k-$21k upfront.
  • First run: 9 options costed; cheapest decent is a 3-bed 1-bath lock-up kit at about $204k-$380k landed, off-grid.
  • Off-grid power is $34k-$64k after the federal battery rebate; install before Dec 2026 to keep the full rate.
  • Roof water covers only 20-30% of a family's use at 400mm rainfall; a bore or river pump is likely needed.
  • Flood planning level at the bridge is about 126.14m AHD; rural land north of the river floods up to 0.5m in a 1% event.
  • Local, factory-built option: Lloyds in Wagga builds a 3-bed 1-bath 'Murrumbidgee' model, 170km away; price by quote only.

Cheapest decent path: a 3-bed 1-bath lock-up kit (Wholesale Homes 'Whitehaven', $52,554 kit) owner-built on a slab, about $120k-$193k for the house plus $84k-$188k to run it off-grid, so roughly $204k-$380k to move in. Shed-home shells land in the same range once windows, walls and fit-out are added; factory-built and relocated homes cost $40k-$100k more. The thing that could sink any option is flood: north of the river the floor must sit at the 1% flood plus 0.5m, which may mean a raised floor and a full DA.

The brief

Darlington Point, NSW 2706 (Murrumbidgee Council)

must have: 3 to 4 bedrooms; 1 kitchen; 1 bathroom; liveable year-round in Riverina heat and frost; runs off-grid: power, water and wastewater on site

nice to have: shed home or similar low-cost build; owner-builder friendly; cheapest total cost to move in, not cheapest kit

Cheapest decent option wins. Always compare the landed, move-in cost: house, freight, slab, labour, fit-out, approvals, plus the off-grid and site works every option needs.

Site and approvals

  • Approval path: A CDC under the Rural Housing Code is barred on flood control lots unless an engineer certifies the footprint is out of floodway and high hazard, so expect a full DA at Darlington Point. (source)
  • Zoning: Land around Darlington Point is almost all RU1 Primary Production; dwellings are controlled by LEP cl 4.2D and the Lot Size Map. The minimum lot size for a dwelling here is unconfirmed. (source)
  • DA fee: Statutory DA fee is about $1,216 for a $250k house, $1,959 for $400k; construction certificate, inspections and s68 septic fees are extra. (source)
  • BASIX: Required before any DA or CDC, off-grid included. Solar counts as an alternative energy system; with no mains, the rain tank must meet the water target. (source)
  • Owner-builder: Permit needed over $10k; over $20k also a course ($290-$550) and White Card. Permit fee $233. One permit per 5 years. Electrical, plumbing and waterproofing still need licensed trades. (source)
  • Owner-builder insurance: No home warranty insurance needed on your own work; contractors doing over $20k must insure. Selling within 7.5 years needs a consumer warning in the contract. (source)
  • Living on site while building: One caravan for your own household needs no s68 approval on your land; a shed is Class 10a and needs a change-of-use approval to live in. (source)
  • Flood planning level: Floor must be at the 1% flood plus 0.5m: about 126.14m AHD at the bridge (gauge 7.78m), varying across the floodplain. (source)
  • Flood risk: No levee north of the river; rural land floods up to 0.5m in a 1% event and 1m in a 0.2% event. Many rural lots are 'flooded isolated submerged'. Warning time from Narrandera is over 4 days. (source)
  • 2022 flood: River peaked near 7m in Oct 2022; the Sturt Hwy near town was closed for weeks, reopening 25 Nov. In 2012 most evacuees were out 5+ days, some 3-7 weeks. (source)
  • Bushfire: Bush fire prone mapping around town is patchy (Category 2 and 3 grassland at sample points). A BAL assessment costs $300-$800. (source)
  • Wastewater rules: Council needs s68 approval to install and a separate approval to operate. Flood-liable land is high risk: yearly inspection and 2-yearly renewal. AWTS must be serviced 4 times a year. (source)
  • Water supply: 175m2 of roof at 400mm catches about 60kL a year against 200-290kL used by 4 people, so a big shed roof into the tanks, a bore ($8k-$25k, water test first) or a river pump under basic landholder rights is needed. (source)
  • Grid or off-grid: Grid is cheaper if an Essential Energy transformer is within about 100-200m ($10k-$33k); past that, or with a new transformer (about $25k more), off-grid at $34k-$64k breaks even before $2k-$3k a year of power bills. (source)
  • Grants: The $10k First Home Owner Grant covers owner-builders if land plus home is $750k or less and you live in it. No owner-builder or Murrumbidgee grant found. (source)

Logistics

  • Oversize loads: Up to 3.5m wide and 26m long, no pilot needed by day; over 3.5m one pilot, over 4.6m two. Sturt Hwy and Kidman Way are on the Class 1 notice. No wide loads 23 Dec-3 Jan. (source)
  • Pilot vehicles: $370-$520 a day each; a one-day oversize move runs $800-$2,500 all in. (source)
  • Cranes: M.I.A. Crane Service (Griffith, Leeton, 15-55t) and A&G Cranes (Griffith, 12-60t). Kits and shed shells need no crane; transportables do. (source)
  • Concrete: HRD Readymix, Mawsons and Murrumbidgee Concrete in Leeton, about 45km; Saunders Concrete has supplied Darlington Point since 1976. (source)
  • Trades: Riverina Outbuild (Ranbuild) and True Blue Sheds in Griffith cover Darlington Point. Trades are thin: a Griffith builder went into administration in Feb 2025 owing locals. (source)
  • Hardware: Bunnings Griffith is 33km, about 25 minutes. (source)

Open questions

  • Does the block have a dwelling entitlement? Ask council for a s10.7 planning certificate (minimum lot size, flood control lot, bush fire prone).
  • Ground level at the house site in m AHD against the flood planning level; decides slab vs raised floor and adds $10k-$40k if raised (estimate).
  • Is the site in flood hazard H3-H6 or 'flooded isolated submerged'? Decides CDC vs DA and dry access.
  • Distance to the nearest Essential Energy transformer, to settle grid vs off-grid.
  • Soil class (geotech $500-$1,500): Riverina clay rated H or E pushes the slab cost up.
  • Quotes to get first: Lloyds Wagga (Murrumbidgee 3 SP), Wholesale Homes freight to 2706, Riverina Outbuild liveable shed.
  • Is there river frontage for a domestic pump, or does water mean a bore?

Crew housing: team members on rotation

  • No option on the block gets under $100k; power, water, septic and approvals add about $45k-$130k to any building.
  • Leasing a 3-bed in Darlington Point or Griffith costs about $10.6k-$21.2k up front, then $430-$530 a week.
  • Darlington Point Accommodation Village: 3-bed, 2-bath cabins with aircon, from $252 a night. No upfront cost.
  • Ex-hire donga camp: 4-room sleeper $19,950 + GST, ensuite unit $31,950 + GST; about $112k-$190k all-in on the block.
  • RU1 allows rural workers' dwellings only on a lot that already has a lawful dwelling house (LEP cl 4.2D).

The best way to house a crew for under $100k is to lease a 3-bed house in Darlington Point or Griffith. Up front it costs about $10.6k-$21.2k (bond, 2 weeks' rent and furniture), then about $28k-$40k a year. The runner-up is the Darlington Point Accommodation Village: nothing up front, 3-bed cabins from $252 a night, about $32k-$46k a year at 50% occupancy. Nothing placed on the block gets under $100k once off-grid power, water, septic and raised footings are counted; the cheapest, an ex-hire donga camp, is about $112k-$190k. What could sink the rental is supply: only 2 houses were listed in Darlington Point, and one of them (1 Corbett St) is now for sale.

to settle:

  • Is there already a lawfully erected dwelling house on the lot? Under Murrumbidgee LEP cl 4.2D a rural workers' dwelling can only be approved on a lot that has one. If not, the first building must be approved as the dwelling house, and the lot must meet the Lot Size Map minimum (cl 4.2C).
  • Ask council: would they approve ex-hire dongas or an expandable (non-Class 1a) unit as a rural workers' dwelling, secondary dwelling (max 120m2) or s68 moveable dwelling? What flood planning level and pier height apply to this lot?
  • How many crew nights a year, and how many people at once? Below about 40% occupancy the Accommodation Village beats a lease; above it, a lease or an on-block camp wins.
  • Will the company sign the lease, and will the landlord accept rotating occupants? Ask the Accommodation Village for a written crew and weekly rate.
  • Can the main house's future solar, battery, tanks and septic be sized now to also serve crew units? Sharing them could cut $30k-$70k from any on-block option.
